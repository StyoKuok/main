My research focuses on how physical agents sense, reconstruct, and reason about the real world under uncertainty. I bridge communication and information theory with learning, geometry, and physics to build data-efficient, physically grounded world models for embodied intelligence.

**Keywords:** Embodied Intelligence & Physical Agents; World Models & Model-Based Learning; 3D Geometry, Reconstruction, and Rendering; Physical & Tactile Sensing; Uncertainty, Information Theory, and Communication; Reinforcement Learning under Physical Constraints. Application domains include micro-robotic systems, legged and mobile robots, and embodied sensing platforms.

My research trajectory started from communication and information theory, where I worked on a UAV-assisted integrated sensing and communication system, grounding my training in uncertainty, representation efficiency, and signal-to-decision pipelines.

From there, my focus gradually shifted from transmission to perception: how sparse, noisy, and partial sensory signals can be reconstructed into meaningful representations of the physical world. This led me to work on tactile sensing, sparse-sensor reconstruction, and learning-based methods for recovering stress fields and geometric structures.

Recently, my interests have converged on learning world models for physical scenes by combining geometry, physics, and uncertainty. This direction spans 3D reconstruction, physically grounded rendering, and representations for fluids and deformable objects. I am particularly interested in the mathematical structure required to model how these scenes evolve, not only how they appear at a single instant.

These representations are foundations for action rather than endpoints. I am interested in how model-based learning, reinforcement learning, and vision-language-action policies can use them to make reliable decisions across varied embodied settings, including magnetically controlled micro-robotic systems and autonomous agents operating under physical constraints.

Rather than focusing on a single material class or robot morphology, I study general physical agents: systems that sense, infer, and act in the real world under uncertainty. My background across communication, learning, geometry, and physical systems allows me to bridge perception, representation, and control, with the long-term goal of enabling more data-efficient and physically grounded embodied intelligence.
